## Here is how business owners in the community are building and refining their own custom GPT estimators:
1. Choose Your Platform You will likely need a paid tier, such as ChatGPT Plus, to create a custom GPT model. Some users recommend using a combination of platforms—for example, building the initial framework on Claude and then using ChatGPT for the final assistant to get the best results.
2. Map Out Your Logic and Upload "Core Knowledge" The AI is only as good as the foundation you give it. Start by mapping out your exact estimating logic. You can then upload documents to train the GPT, including:
• Your specific business details, staffing roles, and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
• Your current price book, or even pricing books you find online.
• Specific billing rules, such as how you charge for travel based on location.
• Note: One user pointed out that the Pro version limits you to uploading 30 pieces of "core knowledge," so it is important to condense your foundational principles and guardrails to fit within that limit.
3. Define the AI's Personality You can program the GPT to communicate in your brand's specific voice. One user told their GPT to have a personality that is "friendly and understanding, but firm and fair".
4. Train it to Analyze Media Once the foundation is built, you can use the assistant to analyze incoming customer requests, including uploaded photos and videos. The GPT can use your uploaded logic to run the math and output an exact scope of work and price within minutes.
5. Create a "Quality Control" Assistant (Optional) To ensure accuracy, one user suggests creating a second, separate AI assistant whose sole job is to evaluate the estimates generated by your primary estimator and double-check the math.
6. Continuously Feed it Real-World Corrections To stop the AI from sounding like a generic robot, you must test and refine it. One user noted that whenever they have to change an AI-generated quote or email, they feed their final version back into the GPT with a prompt like, "FYI, I ended up sending this response instead," so the AI learns to mimic their actual phrasing.
